    Write-up

    Overview

    Our visualization of a global heatmap helps to illustrate projected global climate shifts, specifically answering how precipitation patterns are expected to change under future climate scenarios regarding emissions. It was designed with climate researchers, policy makers, and the general public in mind to help them understand the long term impact of human activities on the environment.

    Data

    This data was retrieved from the NOAA Public CMIP6 Dataset on Google Cloud Marketplace. We mainly focused on using the precipitation, temperature, emissions, and geospatial coordinates variables to help compare a historical baseline against future SSP projection scenarios. The data processing was handled using xarray and pandas to manipulate the multidimensional netCDF files, compute spatial averages, and format the outputs for web rendering. Several different climate models were trained and the data was aggregated together.

    Design Choices

    • Projection & Color Scale: An area-preserving map projection and diverging color scale as the main channel were chosen to intuitively display precipitation increases (e.g., cool toned greens/blues to emphasize wetness) versus decreases (e.g., warm browns to emphasize dryness) without distorting landmass sizes. Hatching is overlaid on specific geographic regions to explicitly indicate model disagreement, ensuring viewer transparency regarding predictive uncertainty.
    • Slider:
    • Fanning Line Chart: A fannning line chart for the place card was implemented to display the expanding spread of model projections for rainfall over time, rather than a single misleading average. It includes a baseline 0% acis line, with a green line signifiying low emission rainfall trends and a red line signifying high emission rainfall trands. A colored fanning area was included to show the range of the percentaged acorss our different climate models.
    • User Interactions: Interactive features (like panning and zooming) allow the user to explore localized geospatial data, demonstrating what the interactions add to the overarching global perspective. User's are able to click on specfic areas or type and search for a specific city, marked on the graph with a point, to be able to see the specific data and predicted rainfall changes.
    • Annotations: We included a legend thats shows the association between color and rainfall change. Coordinates of the location are shown when the user examins the map, and once an area is clicked, information about the location and its mid-century and end of century rainfall predictions are displayed with an explation. Lastly, we included a list of cities where we would see the most change in rainfall due to emissions.

    Key Takeaways

    The key takeaway from our visualization is that human emission choices have a huge impact on global precipitation. Global precipitqtion is expcted to increase over the end of the century, with an even stronger increase if we continue with strong emissions. However, due to increasing global temperatures as well, many regions will actually start to become more dry due to increased evaporation.

    Development Process

    Our development process including splitting the work evenly between all three team members. We first analyzed the dataset and deciding what message we wanted to convey with the final delivarable. Next, each team member generated and explored several static visualizations to understand the patterns in the data. We then focused on making our interactive graph and web page. Making the interactive aspects of the graph was the most time consuming. The interactive visualization was built using standard web technologies (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) alongside D3.js, which was fairly new and difficult for us and took several hours over the week. Overall, our final web page seems to work with little to no errors and is able to clearly convey our message about global warming.
